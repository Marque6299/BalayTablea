Our Story

A teacher's bet on her town's cacao.

How a ₱10,000 performance bonus, a rejected business name, and a stone grinder built Cabatuan's best-known chocolate brand.

The Founder

Catherine Taleon, teacher turned chocolate-maker.

Cacao has grown in Cabatuan since Spanish friars introduced it in the 17th century, when tsokolate was a thick, luxury drink reserved for the elite. The trade faded over generations — until 2012, when the Sangguniang Bayan of Cabatuan officially declared cacao the town's One-Town-One-Product (OTOP) commodity under Resolution No. 030-2012, choosing the brand name "Tan Tono" to honor the town's first gobernadorcillo.

That same year, Catherine Taleon — then a public school teacher — put her own performance bonus toward reviving the craft properly: she chose the traditional metate y mano stone grinder over modern machines, and began training local farmers and out-of-school youth in the process.

She wanted to register the business as "Balay Tablea," but the Department of Trade and Industry rejected the name as too generic. So she reached for something more personal — her childhood pen name — and Sunburst's Balay Tablea was born. Today it's the authorized manufacturer and distributor of Tan Tono Tablea. Catherine's husband, a retired luxury-cruise chef, and her daughter now help run the business alongside her.

The Journey

From a classroom bonus to a national stage.

  1. 2012

    The town chooses cacao

    Cabatuan declares cacao its OTOP commodity under SB Resolution 030-2012. Catherine Taleon invests her performance bonus to found Sunburst's Balay Tablea, choosing hand-grinding over machines.

  2. 2018

    DOST takes notice

    DOST Secretary Fortunato dela Peña visits the workshop; the DOST-VI Small Enterprise Technology Upgrading Program (SETUP) later supports a technology and packaging upgrade.

  3. 2018–19

    Local media & online retail

    Daily Guardian and Iloilo Today profile the business; DOST-assisted tablea with muscovado sugar becomes a top-rated seller on the government's oneSTore PH e-commerce platform.

  4. 2022

    A seat at the table

    Balay Tablea's tablea joins the LaMeza Ilonggo Degustation, placing Cabatuan cacao on a curated Iloilo tasting menu.

  5. 2024

    Cabatuan goes to Macao

    Balay Tablea represents Iloilo at the UNESCO Cities of Gastronomy Expo — its banana-leaf-wrapped rolls sell out before the event ends.

  6. 2025

    Back to Macao, plus a hotel collaboration

    Catherine returns to Macao for cooking demos with Richmonde Hotel Iloilo's Chef Ariel Castañeda Jr. and Megaworld's Chef Panky Lopez. Their earlier collaboration, Tablea Cheesecake with Tultul, is already a hotel signature. Simpol.ph features the brand's story nationally.

  7. Today

    Building what's next

    Over 20 farming families supply cacao monthly. Catherine's next goal: a full Cacao Visitor Center in Cabatuan — part showroom, part classroom.

Community First

The farmers behind every roll.

Sourcing wasn't always steady. Early on, beans arrived under-roasted or over-dried, and Catherine bought them anyway — reasoning that a farmer's sale that day might be their only income, and that she could teach better technique next time rather than turn business away. That patience became a training ground: farmers like Mang Leo of Barangay Ayaman, who once struggled with roasting times, now supply cacao that reliably passes quality checks. Out-of-school youth are paid per piece to hand-wrap each roll in banana leaf, turning a craft step into steady income.
