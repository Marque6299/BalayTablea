From Pod to Cup

Five steps, one unbroken tradition.

No shortcuts, no modern grinders where it counts — just cacao, a stone metate, and a lot of patience.

The Craft

How a cacao pod becomes a roll of tablea.

  1. 01

    Harvest

    Cacao pods are gathered from farms across Cabatuan and the nearby highlands, brought in by more than 20 partner farming families.

  2. 02

    Ferment & Dry

    Beans are fermented and sun-dried by farmers first — a step DOST and DA field training has helped standardize over the years, so batches arrive consistent.

  3. 03

    Roast

    Beans are roasted slow and gently, filling the workshop with a smoky, earthy aroma. Under- or over-roasted batches are still bought — and used as a teaching moment for next time.

  4. 04

    Grind

    Roasted nibs are crushed by hand on the metate y mano — a concave stone slab paired with a handheld stone — the traditional method Catherine chose over modern machines when she started the business.

  5. 05

    Wrap

    Cacao paste is rolled and wrapped in dried banana leaf, which perfumes the chocolate with its own subtle fragrance. Out-of-school youth are trained and paid per piece to do the wrapping.

  6. 06

    Brew

    Whisked into thick, bold tsokolate with a wooden batirol — served the way Ilonggo households always have, alongside mangga't ibos as a welcome merienda.

Backed by Science

Tradition, upgraded — not replaced.

In 2018, DOST Secretary Fortunato dela Peña visited the Cabatuan workshop and encouraged the team to keep refining texture. The DOST Region VI Small Enterprise Technology Upgrading Program (SETUP) followed with a technology and packaging upgrade, helping Balay Tablea meet consistent quality standards while keeping the metate at the center of production.

The result is chocolate that's still ground the old way, but produced, packaged and stored to standards that let it reach hotel kitchens, pasalubong shelves, and international expos.
